01 / About

From learning
to decisions.

How can learning and optimization help us make reliable decisions in a complex, uncertain world?

This workshop brings together researchers working at the intersection of machine learning, mathematical optimization and decision-making. Through invited talks and discussion, we will explore robust learning, optimization under uncertainty, and data-driven approaches to decision-making.

08:30Navid Azizan ↗Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Title: Safe and Reliable Machine Learning under Hard Constraints

Abstract

ML models are increasingly being deployed in domains where respecting safety, physical, or structural constraints is critical. Yet most modern learning systems encode such requirements through data or soft penalties, providing little assurance when deployed beyond the training distribution. In this talk, I will discuss efforts by my group toward building hard constraints directly into learning-based decision-making systems, while retaining the flexibility and scalability that make modern machine learning effective. I will begin with hard-constrained neural networks (HardNets), which add a differentiable constraint-enforcement layer into existing architectures, enabling end-to-end learning under various constraints while preserving expressivity and guaranteeing feasibility. I will then discuss how HardNets can be used to address important problems in dynamical systems and control, including learning chaotic dynamics with boundedness guarantees via energy-constrained operators, as well as safe yet scalable learning-based control and reinforcement learning. Finally, I will turn to modern generative models, namely diffusion and flow-matching models, and show how we can enforce hard constraints on such generative pretrained models by treating sampling as an optimal control problem and imposing constraints on the final generated sample without unnecessarily restricting the intermediate sampling dynamics. I will demonstrate the superior performance of this approach in diverse applications spanning robotic manipulation and planning, PDE boundary control, and language-guided image editing. Together, these advances can pave the way for reliable decision-making systems with intrinsic guarantees.

09:15Daniel Kuhn ↗EPFL

Title: Brenier Meets Adversarial Training: Optimal Transport Geometry for Robust Learning

Abstract

Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) provides a principled framework for learning under distribution shift, but its practical use is hindered by the difficulty of evaluating worst-case risks for nonconvex loss functions. We study a penalized DRO formulation in which the adversary may choose any distribution but incurs a Wasserstein penalty for deviating from the empirical distribution. We show that the adversary’s problem can be reformulated as an optimization problem over transport maps that push empirical samples to adversarial ones, and we prove that optimal maps are cyclically monotone. We also show that standard adversarial training - based on per-sample local optimization - violates cyclical monotonicity and wastes transport costs unless the adversary is severely restricted. We propose two remedies. First, we introduce multi-start particle ascent, which alternates parallel gradient ascent with reassignment to enforce cyclical monotonicity across samples. Second, we parameterize adversarial maps as gradients of input-convex neural networks, which guarantees cyclical monotonicity by construction. Experiments on robust regression and image classification show that our methods consistently outperform standard adversarial training and state-of-the-art baselines, achieving improved robustness and better generalization under distribution shift.

10:20Fredrik D. Johansson ↗Chalmers University of Technology

Title: Meta-learning interpretable machine learning models

Abstract

Interpretable machine learning models that can be inspected, audited, and communicated are critical components of prediction and decision systems based on tabular data. But models based on logical rules, like decision trees and tree ensembles, are still trained one by one without transfer between learning tasks, failing to benefit from similar tasks being solved before. In this talk, I will take a step toward transferable interpretable machine learning (IML) through the idea of the "IML model factory". Our first example is MotherTree, a tabular foundation model trained to generate decision trees in a single inference step, based on a small training set.
